March was all over the place, continuing from [February's terrain ideas](/posts/inspirations/2022-02/). I was thinking about different projects at the same time, gathering inspiration for various ideas that were bouncing around in my head.

I picked up a Playmobil toy that looked like it could be converted into a war machine. I wanted to make it look like a goblin contraption, something cobbled together from scrap. The first image gives off that chaotic vibe I was going for, the second helped me figure out where armor plating should go (metal at the front, wood on the sides), and the third is closer to what I had in mind, though my version won't be as large. There will barely be one level for minis, but that's enough. Years later, writing this in 2026, I still haven't built it. I will one day.

These are from a really good blog called [Rob Hawkins Hobby](https://robhawkinshobby.blogspot.com). He makes great terrain pieces with tons of patience and detail, and his painting is excellent. He also explains his techniques really well. I was particularly impressed by his bone painting method. I screenshotted it to try reproducing it, but honestly, I never had the patience to follow through. The final result looks so good though. I'll use it as inspiration someday.

This is a technique for painting rust. I had some stuff I wanted to make look rusty, so I was looking for a recipe. He starts with all brown, then adds blue-gray to get that metallic bluish look, does a dry brush in bone white to really bring out the edges, and finishes with a brown wash to unify everything. Then he just uses orange for the rust effects. This interested me because I had bought specific rust paints but never really figured out how to use them well. Turns out, rust is basically just orange.

There's a whole blog post with great terrain pieces, but what interested me was how to make trees. I knew I might eventually run a scenario where a tree grows inside a house, and I had already seen this technique of making trees with wire. His is really well done, but way too much work for me. I ended up looking for much simpler methods.

<!-- Image 15 -->
![Stone paintjob with natural gray tones](image-20260410112456879.png)

The paintjob on this really caught my attention. You can really see that these are stones, and yet it's almost all gray. I think there's a bit more going on behind the scenes, but there's something really well done with these stones, similar to the [stone painting techniques I found in February](/posts/inspirations/2022-02/). I really feel like I'm looking at rocks I could see in nature, not the kind of super gray and boring stones I often make. There was something to dig into with this paintjob.

<!-- Image 16 -->
![Barricades made from pencils as sharpened stakes](image-20260410112511190.png)

This isn't the first time I've seen this, but I think seeing it again made me realize I really should craft some barricades. It's something that comes up often in scenarios when goblins want to hide behind cover, or villagers want to protect themselves. And I think there's a very simple way to do it, just by buying pencils in bulk and using them directly as sharpened stakes. That's exactly the kind of thing I enjoy about terrain crafting, and it looks pretty good.

Looking back at all this, I realize March was really about collecting possibilities. None of these projects got finished at the time, but the ideas stuck with me. Some of them I eventually used, others are still waiting for the right moment.
